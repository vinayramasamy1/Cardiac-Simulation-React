# Facilitator Script

Read the **bold quoted text** aloud word for word. Everything else is instructions for you.

*Why a script:* If you phrase things differently each time, you accidentally hint at answers and results become incomparable.

---

## 1. Welcome (3 min)

> **"Thanks for helping today. I'm going to ask you to try out a learning website for cardiac rhythms. This takes about 30 minutes.**
>
> **One important thing: we're testing the website, not you. You can't do anything wrong. If something is confusing, that's the website's fault, and that's exactly what I need to find out.**
>
> **Please think out loud as you go. Tell me what you're looking at, what you expect to happen, and what confuses you. I may not answer questions during the tasks, because I want to see what you'd do on your own, but I'll answer anything at the end.**
>
> **You can stop at any time. Do you have any questions before we start?"**

*Why:* People blame themselves when they struggle and go silent. This tells them their confusion is useful data and primes them to talk.

## 2. Consent (1 min)

> **"With your permission, I'd like to record the screen and your voice so I can review it later. The recording is only used by our team to improve the website and won't be shared publicly. Is that okay?"**

Get a clear yes (written or recorded verbally). If no, take notes only.

*Why:* Recording without consent is a privacy problem, and you may be on the record anyway if this is for a class or organization.

## 3. Warm-up questions (3 min)

Ask these *before* showing the site. Write answers on the notes sheet.

1. **"What's your background with EKG rhythms or emergency medicine?"**
   *Why:* Tells you how to interpret their struggles. A novice confused by jargon is a different finding than a paramedic confused by navigation.
2. **"How do you usually study new material, such as apps, videos, flashcards, or textbooks?"**
   *Why:* Shows what they expect from a learning tool, so you can compare against what the site offers.
3. **"What device are you using today?"**
   *Why:* Layout problems often depend on screen size.

## 4. Tasks (15-18 min)

Say:
> **"I'm going to read you a few short scenarios. Please try to complete each one the way you naturally would. Start from the home page."**

Run tasks from `02-tasks-and-success-criteria.md` in order. For each:
1. Hand over (or read) the scenario. **Do not use the site's button labels in your wording.**
2. Start your timer.
3. Stay quiet. If they ask "is this right?", reply: **"What would you expect?"**
4. If silent for more than ~5 seconds, say: **"What are you thinking right now?"**
5. Stop the task when they succeed, say they're done, give up, or hit the time limit.
6. Ask the quick rating (see below), then reset to the home page.

**Quick rating after every task:**
> **"On a scale from 1 (very hard) to 5 (very easy), how easy was that?"**

*Why:* The gap between what people *do* and what they *say* is itself a finding. Someone can fail a task and still rate it a 4, which tells you they didn't realize they failed.

**When to help:** Only if a participant is stuck for 2+ minutes or visibly upset. Give the smallest nudge, and record the task as "Assisted" so you know it wasn't a true success.

## 5. Post-task interview (4 min)

> **1. "What was the most confusing or frustrating part?"**
> *Why:* Open-ended, so it surfaces problems you didn't predict.
>
> **2. "What did you like most?"**
> *Why:* Tells you what to protect when you make changes.
>
> **3. "If you were studying for a certification, would you use this? Why or why not?"**
> *Why:* Tests whether the site is actually valuable, not just easy.
>
> **4. "Was there anything you expected to find that wasn't there?"**
> *Why:* Reveals missing features and mismatched expectations.

## 6. SUS survey (3 min)

> **"Last, I'll ask you to fill out a short 10-question survey about your overall impression."**

Use `03-sus-survey.md`.

## 7. Close

> **"Thank you, this was very helpful. Do you have any questions for me?"**

Answer any questions you held back. Thank them. Then save the recording and finish your notes **within an hour**, while you still remember.
